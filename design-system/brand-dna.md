# Brand DNA

## Identity in one line

**Cassie Liang builds human-steered systems for attention, memory, and everyday action.**

The website is a record of real work, not a claim of future potential. It should show both how things work and why a person would want them.

## Audience and posture

| Audience | They need to leave believing |
| --- | --- |
| Engineering recruiter | Cassie can ship thoughtful, technically credible product work. |
| Product collaborator | Cassie sees the human consequence of system design and makes clear decisions. |
| Curious peer | There is a real person and a consistent point of view behind the projects. |

Default voice: precise, plainspoken, reflective. Prefer a concrete mechanism over a superlative. Prefer `private beta` or `prototype` over implying a product is shipped.

## Three non-negotiables

1. **Evidence before aesthetic.** Show the interaction, constraint, or technical choice that makes a project real.
2. **People keep the final say.** AI may suggest, surface, organize, or review; it must not quietly decide on a person's behalf.
3. **Care deserves structure.** Work about focus, memory, or daily life must still be concrete about boundaries, states, and tradeoffs.

## Visual personality

- Editorial engineering notebook, not a generic SaaS landing page.
- Warm, composed, and slightly playful; never cute at the expense of clarity.
- High contrast in type scale: tiny operational labels against generous, literary headlines.
- Deliberate asymmetry and visible structure: grids, rules, status marks, and roomy whitespace.
- One interruption per view is enough: a terminal, an offset shadow, or an accent field—not all three competing.

## Color roles

| Token | Meaning | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canvas / Ink | calm, credibility | default page field and all primary text |
| Signal | agency, next action | live state, selection, "now", restrained action emphasis |
| Data | system, evidence | technical artifacts, process, information |
| Memory | reflection, archive, care | personal tools, notes, reflective surfaces |
| Caution | friction that deserves attention | problem framing, process risks, capture/archive work |

Never use the accents merely to distinguish cards. A card earns an accent through its meaning. Use a single accent per component; the rest of the page returns to canvas and ink.

## Typography

| Job | Token | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero and section title | `--cassie-serif` | calm, generous, usually sentence case |
| Body and UI copy | `--cassie-sans` | short paragraphs, comfortable line length |
| Labels, metadata, system states | `--cassie-mono` | small and quiet, never as a body-text substitute |

Serif gives the work emotional weight; mono supplies evidence. Do not turn everything into developer-terminal styling.

## Copy rules

- Lead project descriptions with the user-facing outcome, then name the mechanism.
- Make scope visible: `shipped`, `private beta`, `prototype`, `research`, or `in progress`.
- Use active sentences. Say "turns long webpages into split PDFs," not "a solution for PDF generation."
- Avoid inflated labels: revolutionary, seamless, cutting-edge, best-in-class, AI-powered.
- Bilingual pages must be authored as two natural sentences, not literal word substitution.

## Never do this

- Blue-purple gradients, glass panels, neon, or AI-glow decoration.
- A rainbow grid where every project has an arbitrary color.
- Fake dashboards, fake metrics, or claims beyond the project’s actual state.
- Default browser tables, lists, or blockquotes in public pages.
- Heavy card shadows, pill overload, or animation that competes with reading.
- "AI replaces you" wording or interfaces that hide consequential automation.
